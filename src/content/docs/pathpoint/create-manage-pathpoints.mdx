## Create a Pathpoint [#create-a-pathpoint]

To create a new Pathpoint, click <DNT>**+ Add a flow**</DNT> in the top right of the List View.

This opens the <DNT>**Blank flow**</DNT> dialog. Fill in the following fields:

* <DNT>**Flow name**</DNT> (required): The name displayed on the Pathpoint and in the List View.
* <DNT>**Category**</DNT> (optional): A business category to help organize your Pathpoints, such as E-commerce or Onboarding.
* <DNT>**Description**</DNT> (optional): A short description of the Pathpoint, such as "Purchasing flow for mobile app."
* <DNT>**Refresh data every**</DNT>: How frequently the Pathpoint refreshes its health data. Defaults to 5 minutes.

Click <DNT>**Save**</DNT> to create the Pathpoint. This opens it in edit mode, where you'll build out the structure of your journey.

### Add and configure stages [#add-and-configure-stages]

Edit mode opens with a stage, level, and step already scaffolded. Click on the stage name to rename it.

To add a new stage, click the <DNT>**+**</DNT> icon on a stage header and select <DNT>**Add a stage**</DNT>. New stages are added to the end of the Pathpoint.

To add a KPI directly to a stage, click the <DNT>**+**</DNT> icon and select <DNT>**Add a KPI**</DNT>. See [KPIs](/docs/pathpoint/pathpoint-view#kpis) for more details.

To configure a stage, click the gear icon on the stage header and select <DNT>**Settings**</DNT>. Stage settings include:

* <DNT>**Stage link**</DNT> (optional): Add a URL to provide additional context for the stage, such as a dashboard or document.
* <DNT>**Stage shape**</DNT>: Set the visual shape of the stage to reflect its role in the journey: None, Start, Connect, or Stop.
* <DNT>**Health setup**</DNT>: Choose how the stage health is determined:
  * <DNT>**Rollup of all levels**</DNT>: The stage status reflects the most severe status across all its levels. Use this when you want the stage to react as soon as any level has trouble.
  * <DNT>**Alert policy and conditions**</DNT>: Set up custom alert policies and conditions to determine the health status of the stage. This method lets you define specific conditions that trigger stage health status changes based on alert states. Use this when you want the stage to turn critical based on a stage KPI breaching a threshold.

To delete a stage, click the gear icon and select <DNT>**Delete**</DNT>.

### Add and configure levels & steps [#add-and-configure-levels--steps]

To add a level, click <DNT>**+ Add Level**</DNT> at the bottom of a stage. Levels are automatically numbered based on their position in the stage.

To add a step to a level, click the <DNT>**+**</DNT> icon inside the level. To rename a step, click on the step name and type directly.

To configure a step, click the gear icon on the step and select <DNT>**Settings**</DNT>. Step settings include:

* <DNT>**Step link**</DNT> (optional): Add a URL to provide additional context for the step, such as a dashboard or document.
* <DNT>**Status configuration**</DNT>: Choose how the step health is determined:
  * <DNT>**Exclude step from level status**</DNT>: When checked, the step still displays its own health but doesn't contribute to the level's health rollup.
  * <DNT>**Rollup the worst status**</DNT>: The step health reflects the most severe status of any signal. Optionally enable <DNT>**Apply only when**</DNT> to set a threshold before the status change takes effect.
  * <DNT>**Rollup the best status**</DNT>: The step health reflects the best status of any signal.
  * <DNT>**Included signals**</DNT>: Select which signals are included in step status determination. Defaults to all signals. Signals defined via queries are not included if queries are present.

To add signals to a step, click <DNT>**+ Add signals**</DNT> inside the step. This opens the <DNT>**Select Signals**</DNT> overlay, where you can browse and select the signals that determine the health of the step.

#### Selecting signals manually [#selecting-signals-manually]

Browse or search for signals by name. Check the box next to a signal to add it to the step. To remove a signal, uncheck it in the table or click the x next to it in the <DNT>**Selected signals**</DNT> list.

#### Using dynamic filters [#using-dynamic-filters]

Instead of selecting signals one by one, you can use filters to dynamically target a set of signals. Dynamic filters determine the signals in scope each time the Pathpoint loads — useful when your signal set may change over time.

To use a dynamic filter, apply your desired filter criteria and click <DNT>**Add this filter**</DNT>. The filter definition is added to the <DNT>**Selected signals**</DNT> list along with any signals currently matching it.

To remove a dynamic filter, click the x next to the filter definition in the <DNT>**Selected signals**</DNT> list.

<Callout variant="tip">
  Dynamic filters cannot be edited after creation — to change a filter, remove it and recreate it.
</Callout>

To delete a step, click the gear icon and select <DNT>**Delete**</DNT>. To delete a level, click the x icon next to the level.

### Save your Pathpoint [#save-your-pathpoint]

When you're done building, click <DNT>**Save flow**</DNT> in the top right to save your changes and exit edit mode.

### Edit a Pathpoint [#edit-a-pathpoint]

To edit a Pathpoint, click <DNT>**Edit**</DNT> in the top right of the Pathpoint view. This opens it in edit mode.

In edit mode you can update the Pathpoint's stages, levels, steps, and signals using the same actions described in [Add and configure stages](#add-and-configure-stages) and [Add and configure levels & steps](#add-and-configure-levels--steps) above.

To manage noise when editing a large Pathpoint, use the <DNT>**Collapse all**</DNT> toggle in the top right to hide all signals and show only the Pathpoint structure. This makes it easier to focus on stages, levels, and steps without the distraction of individual signals.

When you're ready, click <DNT>**Save flow**</DNT> in the top right to save your changes and return to the Pathpoint view.

### Resolve editing conflicts [#resolve-editing-conflicts]

More than one person can edit a Pathpoint at the same time. If someone else saves while you're still editing, a <DNT>**Changes were made by another user**</DNT> dialog appears when you try to save. It names the person who saved and gives you two options:

* <DNT>**Preview changes**</DNT>: Shows you their version so you can decide how to proceed.
* <DNT>**Cancel**</DNT>: Returns you to edit mode with your changes intact.

In preview, a banner gives you three ways forward:

* <DNT>**Exit preview**</DNT>: Returns you to edit mode with your changes intact.
* <DNT>**Overwrite changes**</DNT>: Saves your version, replacing theirs.
* <DNT>**Accept changes and refresh**</DNT>: Discards your changes and loads their version.

<Callout variant="tip">
  The API applies the same check through the flow's `version` field. See [Update a flow](/docs/apis/nerdgraph/examples/nerdgraph-pathpoint-flows/#update-flow).
</Callout>

### Duplicate a Pathpoint [#duplicate-a-pathpoint]

To duplicate a Pathpoint, click the <DNT>**...**</DNT> menu on any row in the List View and select <DNT>**Duplicate**</DNT>. It's also available in edit mode from the <DNT>**...**</DNT> menu next to <DNT>**Save flow**</DNT>.

This opens the <DNT>**Duplicate a flow**</DNT> dialog. You can update the following before saving:

* <DNT>**Flow name**</DNT> (required): Defaults to the original name with "Copy" appended.
* <DNT>**Select an account**</DNT>: The account to create the duplicate in. Defaults to the current account, so use this to copy a Pathpoint into a different account.
* <DNT>**Category**</DNT>: Inherited from the original Pathpoint.
* <DNT>**Description**</DNT>: Inherited from the original Pathpoint.
* <DNT>**Refresh data every**</DNT>: Inherited from the original Pathpoint.

Choose what to duplicate:

* <DNT>**Add Structure (stages, levels, steps)**</DNT>: Copies the stages, levels, and steps only.
* <DNT>**Everything (structure, signals, flow KPIs)**</DNT>: Copies the structure along with its signals.

Click <DNT>**Save**</DNT> to create the duplicate.

## General settings [#general-settings]

General settings provides a central place to view and update the core properties of a Pathpoint. To access it, select <DNT>**General settings**</DNT> from the top navigation within a Pathpoint.

### Basic information [#basic-information]

Click <DNT>**Edit**</DNT> to update the following properties:

* <DNT>**Flow name**</DNT>: The name displayed on the Pathpoint and in the List View.
* <DNT>**Category**</DNT>: The business category associated with the Pathpoint.
* <DNT>**Description**</DNT>: A short description of the Pathpoint.
* <DNT>**Refresh data every**</DNT>: How frequently the Pathpoint refreshes its health data.

General settings also shows who created the Pathpoint and when, and who last edited it.

### Contacts [#contacts]

Add emails or Slack channels to keep your team connected to this Pathpoint. Click <DNT>**Add contact**</DNT> to add a contact.

### Links [#links]

Add runbooks, repositories, and other relevant documents to provide additional context for the Pathpoint. Click <DNT>**Add links**</DNT> to add a link.

### Delete a Pathpoint [#delete-a-pathpoint]

To delete a Pathpoint, click <DNT>**Delete Pathpoint**</DNT> in the bottom right of General Settings.

<Callout variant="caution">
  Deleting a Pathpoint is permanent and cannot be undone.
</Callout>

## Access permissions [#access-permissions]

What you can do in Pathpoint depends on two things: the permissions your role grants you, and the accounts you have access to.

### Role-based access control [#role-based-access-control]

Pathpoint has its own capability in the permissions list, under <DNT>**New Relic One**</DNT>. It's account-scoped, so a user's Pathpoint access applies to the accounts their role covers.

The <DNT>**Pathpoint**</DNT> capability has three permissions:

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Permission</th>
      <th>Grants access to</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td><DNT>**Read**</DNT></td>
      <td>Viewing Pathpoints and their health data</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><DNT>**Modify**</DNT></td>
      <td>Creating Pathpoints and editing existing ones, including their stages, levels, steps, signals, and KPIs</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><DNT>**Delete**</DNT></td>
      <td>Deleting Pathpoints</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

To grant these permissions, an organization admin adds them to a [custom role](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/new-relic-one-user-management/account-user-mgmt-tutorial/#roles) and assigns that role to a group. To find the roles UI, go to <DNT>**[one.newrelic.com](https://one.newrelic.com/all-capabilities)**</DNT>, click the [user menu](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/general-account-settings/intro-account-settings), then go to <DNT>**Administration > Access management > Roles**</DNT>.

### Data access [#data-access]

Data access is best understood as the intersection of your individual account access permissions and Pathpoint's account access permissions.

For example, if your organization has 10 accounts in New Relic and you have access to all 10, but Pathpoint is only enabled on 2 of those accounts, the data available to you in Pathpoint will be limited to those 2 accounts.

If the reverse is true — you have access to 2 accounts but Pathpoint has access to all 10 — the data will still be limited to your 2 accounts. Each user sees data only for the accounts they have access to.

Because different users may have access to different data scopes, a Pathpoint may contain data from accounts you don't have access to. In these cases, a <DNT>**No access**</DNT> indicator appears at the top of the affected stage to let you know you're only seeing partial data within that stage.
