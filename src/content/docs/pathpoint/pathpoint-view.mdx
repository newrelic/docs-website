## Pathpoint view [#pathpoint-view]

<img
  title="Pathpoint view"
  alt="A screenshot showing the Pathpoint view."
  src="/images/pathpoint-detail-view.webp"
/>

The Pathpoint view shows the active health status of a Pathpoint, and is designed to make it simple to identify the areas experiencing issues in the journey.

To open the Pathpoint view, click any row from the List View. See [List View](/docs/pathpoint/get-started-pathpoint#list-view) for more.

<Callout variant="tip">
  The Pathpoint view reflects the live health status of your signals, refreshed at the interval set for the Pathpoint.
</Callout>

### KPI bar [#kpi-bar]

At the top of the Pathpoint view, the <DNT>**KPI bar**</DNT> displays the key business metrics configured for this Pathpoint — such as order fulfillment time, cart abandonment, or revenue. These give you immediate context on the business impact of what you're seeing below.

KPIs can also be associated with individual stages. To view the KPIs for a specific stage, click the trend icon on the stage header.

Each KPI is evaluated over the time window set in its own query, so the KPI bar has no time picker. See [Add a KPI](#add-a-kpi) for how to set that window.

### Stages and steps [#stages-and-steps]

The main area of the Pathpoint view shows each stage of the journey as a column. Each stage header is color-coded to reflect its current health: green (healthy), yellow (warning), or red (critical). This lets you immediately identify which part of the journey is experiencing issues.

For more on how health is calculated, see [Health indicators](#health-indicators).

### Levels, steps and signals [#levels-steps-signals]

Within each stage, signals are organized into steps, and steps are grouped into numbered levels. Each step shows a summary of its signal health. Click a step to expand it and see the individual signals contributing to its health. A signal is either an entity, such as a workload or a database, or an alert condition.

If a stage displays a <DNT>**Signals not reporting**</DNT> warning, some signals in that stage appear to be missing. Click <DNT>**View details**</DNT> for details.

### Filtering the view [#filtering-the-view]

Use the toggles in the top right of the Pathpoint view to focus on what matters:

* <DNT>**Expand critical only**</DNT>: Expands all steps with signals in a critical state. Warning, healthy, and unknown signals are collapsed or hidden. Steps excluded from the level status rollup are hidden even if they're in a critical state.
* <DNT>**Expand unhealthy only**</DNT>: Expands all steps with signals in a warning or critical state. Healthy and unknown signals are collapsed or hidden. Steps excluded from the level status rollup are hidden even if they're in a warning or critical state.
* <DNT>**Expand all**</DNT>: Expands all levels across all stages at once, showing all steps and signals regardless of their health state.

### Investigating a problem [#investigating-a-problem]

As signals experience problems, their status in the Pathpoint updates to reflect the severity of the issue. By grouping signals into stages and steps, Pathpoint makes it easy to evaluate the potential impact of a problem and isolate where in the journey it's occurring. See [Health indicators](#health-indicators) for more information on how health status is determined.

When a signal is showing as warning or critical, click on it to open the signal detail panel. This immediately shows you an entity overview with its golden signals that can help pinpoint where the problem is manifesting.

Click <DNT>**View details**</DNT> to open the entity-specific view, including Golden Signals that can help pinpoint where the problem is manifesting.

By surfacing these details directly in context, Pathpoint simplifies problem identification and drives you quickly to next steps in an investigation.

### Signal error indicators [#signal-error-indicators]

The signals that make up a Pathpoint can change over time. When Pathpoint detects an issue with the signals in a stage, a warning indicator appears at the top of the affected stage.

There are two signal warnings you may encounter:

* <DNT>**Missing signals**</DNT>: A signal that was included in the Pathpoint no longer exists in New Relic. Click the warning icon to see the list of missing signals for that stage. In the modal, click <DNT>**Update signals**</DNT> to open the signals overlay and make the necessary adjustments.
* <DNT>**No access**</DNT>: The Pathpoint includes signals you don't have access to. This indicator is purely informational — it lets you know you're seeing only a subset of data in the Pathpoint. For more information, see [Data access](/docs/pathpoint/create-manage-pathpoints#data-access).

## Health indicators [#health-indicators]

Pathpoint uses the same health indicators you see across the New Relic platform. This section covers what each status means and how it rolls up through a Pathpoint.

### Health status [#health-status]

Health status in New Relic is fundamentally derived from alert definitions. There are four possible states:

* <DNT>**Red (Critical)**</DNT>: A critical incident is currently open on the entity. For entities, this reflects the worst case — if even one attached alert condition is in a critical state, the entity is red regardless of the state of other attached conditions.
* <DNT>**Yellow (Warning)**</DNT>: A warning incident is currently open, with no critical incidents open.
* <DNT>**Green (Healthy)**</DNT>: No active incidents for the entity.
* <DNT>**Grey (Unknown)**</DNT>: Indicates one of two situations: there's no data reporting for the entity, or no alert conditions have been defined on the entity (meaning no health parameters exist for that entity).

<Callout variant="tip">
  Service Levels are an exception to the alert-based health rule — the status of a Service Level may also be set based on its compliance level, even if no alert definitions have been defined for it.
</Callout>

### How health is determined [#how-health-is-determined]

Pathpoint derives health in two ways:

* <DNT>**Technical health**</DNT>: The most severe status rolled up through the hierarchy — signals into steps, steps into levels, levels into stages, and stages into the Pathpoint.
* <DNT>**Business health**</DNT>: The most severe status across the alert conditions on your KPIs.

You choose which one applies to the Pathpoint as a whole, and to each stage individually. The choices are independent, so a Pathpoint can use business health while the stages inside it use technical health, or the reverse.

#### Technical health [#technical-health]

Technical health propagates bottom-up: Signal → Step → Level → Stage → Pathpoint.

* <DNT>**Signal**</DNT>: The base health unit. Each signal reflects its current health state based on active incidents.
* <DNT>**Step**</DNT>: Reflects the most severe status of its signals by default. You can switch it to reflect the best status instead, apply a threshold before the status changes, and include only selected signals. Steps can also be excluded from contributing to their level's health. See [Add and configure levels and steps](/docs/pathpoint/create-manage-pathpoints#add-and-configure-levels--steps) for more info.
* <DNT>**Level**</DNT>: Always reflects the most severe status of its underlying steps. Not configurable. Steps excluded from the health rollup don't affect the level status.
* <DNT>**Stage**</DNT>: When set to <DNT>**Rollup of all levels**</DNT>, the stage reflects the most severe status across all its levels.
* <DNT>**Pathpoint**</DNT>: When set to <DNT>**Use stage health**</DNT>, the Pathpoint reflects the most severe status of its stages. You can include all stages, or select specific stages only.

#### Business health [#business-health]

Business health comes from the alert conditions on your KPIs instead of the signal rollup. Use it when status should follow a KPI breaching a threshold — a checkout Pathpoint can turn red because conversion rate dropped, even while every underlying service is healthy.

* At the Pathpoint level, select <DNT>**Use custom alerts**</DNT> in <DNT>**Health settings**</DNT>.
* At the stage level, select <DNT>**Alert policy and conditions**</DNT> in the stage's <DNT>**Settings**</DNT>.

### Unknown health status [#unknown-health-status]

If all signals in a step are grey (unknown), the step shows as grey. If a step has a mix of grey and non-grey signals, the grey signals drop out of the health rollup and the most severe non-grey status applies instead.

### Example [#example]

Consider a checkout Pathpoint with two stages. The Login stage contains a level with an <DNT>**Email Verification**</DNT> step that has an active critical incident — the step turns red, the level turns red, and the stage turns red. The Browse stage is healthy and shows green. If the Pathpoint rolls up from all stages, its status turns red because the most severe status takes precedence.

### Configure Pathpoint health [#configure-pathpoint-health]

To set how a Pathpoint's overall health status is determined, select <DNT>**Health settings**</DNT> from the navigation within a Pathpoint.

Under <DNT>**Pathpoint health**</DNT>, choose one of two options:

* <DNT>**Use stage health**</DNT>: Technical health. The Pathpoint status reflects the most severe health status across its stages. Click <DNT>**Configure**</DNT> to choose which stages are included in the rollup:
  * <DNT>**All stages**</DNT>: The Pathpoint status changes if any stage starts having trouble.
  * <DNT>**Select stages only**</DNT>: The Pathpoint status changes only if specific stages start having trouble. Select the stages to include from the list.
* <DNT>**Use custom alerts**</DNT>: Business health. Set up alert policies and conditions to determine the health status of the Pathpoint. Use this when the Pathpoint should turn red on a KPI breaching a threshold rather than on the underlying stage rollup.

The <DNT>**Preview**</DNT> panel on the right shows the resulting Pathpoint status based on your configuration.

## KPIs [#kpis]

The <DNT>**KPIs**</DNT> page gives you a dedicated view to analyze the business metrics configured for your Pathpoint. To access it, select <DNT>**KPIs**</DNT> from the navigation within a Pathpoint.

The KPIs page displays all KPIs configured for your Pathpoint as time series charts, giving you a deeper view into how your key business metrics are trending over time.

Each KPI chart shows a time series visualization of how the metric has trended over time. Use the time picker in the top right to adjust the time range of the chart — for example, the last 30 minutes or the last hour.

KPIs are grouped by scope — <DNT>**Global KPIs**</DNT> apply to the whole Pathpoint, while stage-level KPIs are associated with a specific stage.

To manage your KPIs, click <DNT>**Manage KPIs**</DNT> in the top right.

### Manage KPIs [#manage-kpis]

The <DNT>**Manage KPIs**</DNT> page is where you create, edit, and delete the KPIs for your Pathpoint. It shows a table of all configured KPIs with their name, category, description, and associated stage.

#### Add a KPI [#add-a-kpi]

To add a new KPI, fill in the following fields in the <DNT>**Add a KPI**</DNT> section:

* <DNT>**KPI name**</DNT> (required): A descriptive, business-friendly name for the metric.
* <DNT>**Category**</DNT> (optional): Classify the KPI by functional area, such as Financial Performance or Growth.
* <DNT>**KPI applies to**</DNT>: Choose whether the KPI applies to the whole Pathpoint (Global) or to a specific stage. Stage-level KPIs are associated with that stage and can be viewed by clicking the trend icon on the stage header in the Pathpoint view.
* <DNT>**Description**</DNT> (optional): A short description of what the KPI measures.
* <DNT>**Query**</DNT>: The NRQL query used to calculate the KPI. Click <DNT>**Preview**</DNT> to validate the query and confirm it returns the expected result before saving.

Click <DNT>**Save**</DNT> to add the KPI.

#### Edit, duplicate or delete a KPI [#edit-duplicate-delete-kpi]

To edit, duplicate, or delete a KPI, click the <DNT>**...**</DNT> menu on the KPI's row in the table and select <DNT>**Edit**</DNT>, <DNT>**Duplicate**</DNT>, or <DNT>**Delete**</DNT>.

<Callout variant="caution">
  Deleting a KPI is permanent and cannot be undone.
</Callout>
