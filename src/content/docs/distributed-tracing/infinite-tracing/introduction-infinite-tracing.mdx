Infinite Tracing is a fully managed cloud-based solution that can analyze 100% of your trace data and choose the most actionable data, letting you investigate and solve issues quickly.

This document only applies to the Infinite Tracing feature. If you are looking for our standard distributed tracing solution, see the [standard distributed tracing setup guide](/docs/distributed-tracing/concepts/quick-start).

## What is Infinite Tracing? [#understand-infinite-tracing]

Infinite Tracing allows you to send all your trace data to our cloud-based service and rely on Infinite Tracing to make sampling decisions. You can configure Infinite Tracing in various ways to ensure it's keeping the trace data you need to see.

Infinite Tracing requires you to send your telemetry to a [trace observer](/docs/distributed-tracing/infinite-tracing/set-trace-observer), a dedicated, regional ingestion endpoint that buffers your spans in the cloud. The trace observer is the entry point for all Infinite Tracing data, and it accepts telemetry from:

* New Relic <InlinePopover type="apm"/> agents, via gRPC
* [OpenTelemetry](/docs/opentelemetry/get-started/opentelemetry-get-started-intro) collectors and SDKs, via [OTLP over gRPC or HTTP](/docs/distributed-tracing/infinite-tracing/set-trace-observer#apm-and-third-party)
* Other tools, such as Zipkin, via the [Trace API](/docs/distributed-tracing/trace-api/introduction-trace-api)

Unlike our standard distributed tracing options, Infinite Tracing can process more trace data. It uses [tail-based sampling](/docs/distributed-tracing/concepts/how-new-relic-distributed-tracing-works#tail-based) (sampling after data is collected), as opposed to the head-based sampling that our standard tracing feature uses. Because the sampling decision is made after all the spans in a trace have arrived, Infinite Tracing can keep the traces you need for troubleshooting, such as traces with errors or unusually slow traces, instead of deciding at the start of a request, before you know whether it will fail or be slow. In addition to these targeted samplers, Infinite Tracing also uses a percentage-based [random trace filter](/docs/distributed-tracing/infinite-tracing/infinite-tracing-configure-random-trace-filter) to keep a representative sample of all the traces it observes (1% by default).

Resources for learning more about Infinite Tracing:

* [Learn the technical details of Infinite Tracing sampling and architecture](/docs/distributed-tracing/concepts/how-new-relic-distributed-tracing-works#sampling)

## Requirements [#requirements]

Requirements differ depending on your [pricing model](/docs/accounts/original-accounts-billing/original-product-based-pricing/overview-pricing-models):

* Usage-based pricing model: requires Pro or Enterprise edition.
* Original pricing model: requires New Relic help to enable it for your organization. For questions, contact your New Relic account representative.

The ability to set up this feature is governed by [Infinite Tracing permissions](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/new-relic-one-user-management/user-permissions#infinite-tracing).

## Enable Infinite Tracing [#enable-infinite-tracing]

When enabling Infinite Tracing, you should ideally enable it for all associated services. If some services in a trace use Infinite Tracing and others use our standard tracing solutions, each service makes its own sampling decision: standard tracing samples at the start of a request, while Infinite Tracing waits for the complete trace. As a result, spans from standard tracing services may be missing, which causes fragmented traces, orphaned spans, and inaccurate trace metrics. For details, see [trace configuration conflicts](/docs/distributed-tracing/troubleshooting/infinite-tracing-trace-configuration-conflicts).

To get started, follow the [trace observer setup guide](/docs/distributed-tracing/infinite-tracing/set-trace-observer).

## Configure Infinite Tracing [#configure]

After enabling Infinite Tracing, you can [configure trace observer filtering](/docs/distributed-tracing/infinite-tracing/set-trace-observer#follow-up-config) to ensure it retains the exact data you need.
