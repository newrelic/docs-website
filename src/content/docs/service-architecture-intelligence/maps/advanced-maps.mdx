<DNT>**Maps**</DNT> provides a unified and integrated view of your cloud estate, allowing seamless navigation and exploration of services and infrastructure. It integrates tracing, infrastructure, and performance data into a cohesive experience, addressing limitations of the <DNT>**[Service map](/docs/new-relic-solutions/new-relic-one/ui-data/service-maps/service-maps/)**</DNT> experiences.

* **Comprehensive visualization**: View your entire digital landscape that shows all your services and infrastructure in one place. This visualization helps you quickly grasp complex architectures and make informed decisions about your cloud environment.

* **Automatic Resource Detection**: With [auto-discovery](/docs/infrastructure/amazon-integrations/connect/set-up-auto-discovery-of-aws-entities/), <DNT>Maps</DNT> automatically identifies new resources and their relationships. This feature ensures your data is always current, reducing the time and effort needed to manually update and track changes in your cloud estate.

## Availability [#availability]

<DNT>Maps</DNT> is available for all [<DNT>Full platform users</DNT>](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/new-relic-one-user-management/user-type/#user-type-capabilities). On the [<DNT>Data + Core Compute</DNT>](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/new-relic-one-pricing-billing/new-relic-one-pricing-billing/) pricing model, all users are <DNT>Full platform users</DNT> and have access to <DNT>Maps</DNT>.  <DNT>Core</DNT> and <DNT>Basic</DNT> users in a User pricing model continue to get the old maps experience.

If you're a <DNT>Full platform user</DNT> and want to switch back to the old maps experience, click <DNT>**Switch back for now**</DNT> from the map view.

## Roles and permissions [#roles-permissions]

Maps is a read-only experience. All [<DNT>Full platform users</DNT>](/docs/accounts/accounts-billing/new-relic-one-user-management/user-type/#user-type-capabilities) can access Maps without any additional roles.

<Callout variant="important">
There are no create, update, or delete operations available within Maps. For more information on the overall SAI role model, refer to [SAI Getting Started](/docs/service-architecture-intelligence/getting-started/#roles-permissions).
</Callout>

## Visualize relationships [#visualize-relationships]

<DNT>**Maps**</DNT> uses different visual patterns to represent relationships between entities, depending on the type of relationship. This keeps the map readable as your architecture grows: primary relationships stay visible on the canvas, while secondary details remain available on demand. To see these patterns applied to a specific trace, refer to [Understand the trace details UI page](/docs/distributed-tracing/ui-data/trace-details/).

<CollapserGroup>
    <Collapser
      id="line-relationship"
      title="Line between two entities"
      >
      A directed line with an arrow is the default way <DNT>**Maps**</DNT> represents a connection between two entities, showing the direction of data flow. For example, if <DNT>Service A</DNT> calls <DNT>Service B</DNT>, an arrow points from <DNT>Service A</DNT> to <DNT>Service B</DNT>. Most service-to-service relationships on the map use this pattern.
    </Collapser>
    <Collapser
      id="boundary-box"
      title="Boundary boxes"
      >
      Some relationships represent containment rather than data flow. For example, a database cluster contains its instances, and a <DNT>Kubernetes</DNT> deployment contains pods. Representing containment with a directional line would incorrectly imply data flow where there is only structure.

      For entities with a <DNT>**Contains**</DNT> relationship, <DNT>**Maps**</DNT> automatically groups them inside a boundary box, with the parent entity visually wrapping its children. This shows the hierarchy without requiring you to read labels or follow arrows, and reduces the number of edges on the map.
    </Collapser>
    <Collapser
      id="popover-detail"
      title="Detail on the entity popover"
      >
      Some relationships fan out too widely to draw as individual lines without cluttering the map. For example, multiple <DNT>Kafka</DNT> brokers can manage a single <DNT>Kafka</DNT> topic. Drawing a line to every broker would make the topic look more complex than it is.

      For these <DNT>**Is managed by**</DNT> relationships, point to the entity to view the full relationship detail in its popover, instead of adding permanent lines to the map.
    </Collapser>
    <Collapser
      id="overlay-icon"
      title="Overlay icon"
      >
      Some relationships are relevant but secondary, and apply to nearly every entity. For example, most services have a service level attached to them. Drawing a line from every service to its service level would dominate the map with edges.

      For these relationships, <DNT>**Maps**</DNT> shows a small overlay icon on the entity node instead of a line. The icon for a [service level](/docs/licenses/license-information/referenced-policies/service-level-availability-commitment/) relationship appears only when that service level is alerting. Point to the icon to view the relationship details.
    </Collapser>
</CollapserGroup>

## Map entry points [#entry-points]

<DNT>**Maps**</DNT> provides a consistent mapping experience accessible through various entry points. Depending on the type of entity you select to explore, separate views are available to show services, teams, infrastructure resources, entity relationships in a workload, or dynamic data flows. This unified approach offers clear insights into service interactions, infrastructure dependencies, team operations, and data flows.

<CollapserGroup>
    <Collapser
      id="service-map"
      title="Visualize service interactions"
      >
      <DNT>**Maps**</DNT> enables you to visualize relationships and dependencies among services within mobile, browser, and APM environments. In its default view, the map displays only the services. Selecting a service node reveals its associated infrastructure resources in the bottom panel.

      To access the map for mobile, browser, and APM environments:

      1. Go to <DNT>**[one.newrelic.com](https://one.newrelic.com/all-capabilities)**</DNT>.
      2. From the capability list, select either <DNT>**APM & services**</DNT>, <DNT>**Mobile**</DNT>, or <DNT>**Browser**</DNT>.
      3. From the service list, search and select the service you want to view.
      4. To open the map, from the second-left nav, click <DNT>**Service map**</DNT>.

To access the map for a service from an alert:
  1. Go to <DNT>**[one.newrelic.com > Alerts](https://one.newrelic.com/alerts)**</DNT>.
  2. To open the issue page, from <DNT>**Issues & Activity**</DNT>, select the issue that is associated with a service (Check the <DNT>**Entity name**</DNT> column).
  3. To get the service map, from the issue page, scroll to the <DNT>**Impacted entries**</DNT> section.

    </Collapser>

With [AWS Auto-Discovery](/docs/infrastructure/amazon-integrations/connect/set-up-auto-discovery-of-aws-entities), you can also view the internal relationships between AWS services.

      To access the Infrastructure map:

      1. Go to <DNT>**[one.newrelic.com](https://one.newrelic.com/alerts) > All entities**</DNT>.
      2. From the <DNT>**All entities**</DNT> page, select the host you want to view.
      3. To open the map, on the host page, click <DNT>**Infrastructure**</DNT>.

    <Collapser
      id="group-services"
      title="Group nodes to organize the map view"
      >
      You can group nodes in the map to reduce clutter and highlight relationships, making it easier to identify patterns and manage dependencies. This structured approach provides a clear and organized view of your cloud environment, tailored to your specific needs.

      ### Create groups [#create-groups]

      1. To organize nodes by a specific attribute in the map, go to the <DNT>**Group by**</DNT> drop-down and select the attribute.
      2. To get the grouping on the map, click <DNT>**Apply**</DNT>. Each group displays the number of nodes it contains.
      3. To add another level to the group, from the <DNT>**Group by**</DNT> drop-down, click <DNT>**+ Create a group**</DNT> > select an attribute > <DNT>**Apply**</DNT>. The first level attribute forms the primary group, and the second level attribute forms sub-groups nested in the primary group. You can apply up to five levels of grouping.

      #### Group appearance and naming [#group-appearance]

      Groups are named based on the selected attribute. For example, if you are grouping the nodes by the `Account` attribute, the group name appears as `Account: <account name>`. Groups are color-coded according to the health status of the nodes within them, as follows:
      * **Red**: At least one critical node
      * **Yellow**: At least one degraded node but no critical nodes

      Select a group to view the details of the grouped entities in a table at the right panel. You can also expand groups to view the nodes and nested sub-groups within them. As per your requirement you can reorder group levels by shuffling attributes in the <DNT>**Group by**</DNT> drop-down.
