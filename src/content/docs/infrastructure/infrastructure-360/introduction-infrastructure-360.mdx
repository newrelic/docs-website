<DNT>**Infrastructure 360**</DNT> automatically discovers the infrastructure in your cloud accounts, maps the relationships between your resources, and tracks the configuration changes across them. Together, these give you a current picture of what you're running, how well you're monitoring it, how it connects, and what's changing across your environment.

## Key capabilities [#key-capabilities]

<DNT>Infrastructure 360</DNT> gives you the following capabilities:

* **Reliability coverage**: Auto-discover the resources in your connected cloud accounts without first setting up metric streaming or polling, so your inventory stays current as your environment changes, and see how well you're monitoring each resource at a glance. Guided nudges help you close gaps, such as connecting a cloud account or installing an agent where you haven't fully instrumented a resource.

  <Callout variant="tip">
    Auto-discovery creates entities and relationships, not telemetry. A discovered resource gets an entity, tags, its cloud configuration, and a place on your maps, but you still need to connect metrics or install an agent to get charts and alerts for it.
  </Callout>

* **Relationships**: See the relationships between your resources, so you can understand how your applications and infrastructure connect and depend on one another, and trace impact back to a shared dependency when something degrades.
* **Configuration changes**: Track configuration changes across your discovered resources (what changed and when) with a configuration explorer and a change-tracking timeline on each entity.
* **One connected view**: See all of your infrastructure in one place (cloud resources, hosts, <DNT>Kubernetes</DNT>, and databases) across the New Relic accounts you have access to, and bring the alerts, cost, and security signals for your infrastructure together in a single view.

## How it works [#how-it-works]

<DNT>Infrastructure 360</DNT> discovers the resources in the cloud accounts and subscriptions you connect, and combines them with the hosts, <DNT>Kubernetes</DNT> clusters, and databases New Relic already monitors. As your environment changes, discovery keeps that picture up to date and highlights where you can improve your monitoring coverage.
