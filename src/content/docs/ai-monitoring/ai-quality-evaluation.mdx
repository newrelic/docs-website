AI Quality Evaluation is a feature related to New Relic AI Monitoring that helps enable you to assess the quality and safety of inputs and outputs for the LLMs you would like to monitor. By applying a set of configurable quality metrics, you can use AI Quality Evaluation to help identify problematic responses, detect potential security threats, and gain confidence in the behavior of your AI-powered applications.

Evaluations run automatically on a sample of your ingested AI Monitoring data using AI identified APM entities. Evaluations apply to all APM entities in the account. An option to exclude specific entities will be available in a future release. AI Quality Evaluation uses an LLM-as-a-judge approach—where a dedicated evaluation model scores each interaction against the metrics you configure.



## How it works [#how-it-works]

Once enabled, AI Quality Evaluation runs as follows:

1. **Data ingestion**: Your AI application sends traces to New Relic via AI Monitoring (using the APM agents).
2. **Sampling**: A configurable sampling rate determines what percentage of messages are evaluated.
3. **Evaluation**: Each sampled message is scored against your selected metrics by a judge model.
4. **Flagging**: Messages with scores within your configured range are flagged for review.
5. **Visibility**: Results appear in a dedicated Evaluation UI and are linked to the AI trace, so you can investigate flagged interactions in context.

## Quality metrics [#quality-metrics]

The following quality metrics are available in the first release. Each metric produces a score between 0.0 and 1.0. Score interpretation varies by metric type:

- **Security and safety metrics** (Bias, Toxicity, PII Leakage, Prompt Injection, Jailbreak): A higher score indicates a higher severity of violation. A score of `0.0` means no violation, `0.5` is an average violation, and `1.0` is a critical violation.
- **Response accuracy metrics** (Answer Relevancy, Faithfulness, Contextual Relevancy): A lower score indicates a higher severity of violation. A score of `0.0` is a critical violation, `0.5` is an average violation, and `1.0` means no violation.

Additional metrics are planned for future releases.

## Configuring ranges [#configuring-ranges]

Each metric has a configurable score range—a minimum and maximum value between 0.0 and 1.0—that determines when a message is flagged. A message is flagged when its score falls within the configured range.

Because security/safety metrics and response accuracy metrics use inverted score scales, the direction of flagging differs:

- **Security and safety metrics** (Bias, Toxicity, PII Leakage, Prompt Injection, Jailbreak): Flag higher scores. For example, a Toxicity range of `0.7`–`1.0` flags messages where the judge model has at least 70% confidence the content may be toxic.
- **Response accuracy metrics** (Answer Relevancy, Faithfulness, Contextual Relevancy): Flag lower scores. For example, an Answer Relevancy range of `0.0`–`0.2` flags messages where the model's answer is likely irrelevant to the user's question.

To start, it may be beneficial to use the preset ranges and adjust based on the volume and accuracy of flagged results in your environment.

## Enable AI Quality Evaluation [#enable]

### Prerequisites [#prerequisites]

- You must have AI Monitoring enabled for your application.
- Your AI application must be instrumented with a supported New Relic APM agent.

### Steps [#steps]

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to <DoNotTranslate>**[one.newrelic.com](https://one.newrelic.com) > AI Monitoring > Settings**</DoNotTranslate>.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Under <DoNotTranslate>**Sampling**</DoNotTranslate>, set the percentage of ingested messages to evaluate.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Toggle on the metrics you want to evaluate. You can enable one or more metrics independently. Each metric comes with a preset range as a starting point.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Adjust the score range for each enabled metric if needed (minimum and maximum values between `0.0` and `1.0`).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Click <DoNotTranslate>**Save**</DoNotTranslate>.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## View evaluation results [#view-results]

Go to <DoNotTranslate>**[one.newrelic.com](https://one.newrelic.com) > AI Monitoring > LLM Evaluation**</DoNotTranslate> to see evaluation results. The <DoNotTranslate>**Quality Metrics**</DoNotTranslate> tab gives you a live picture of how your AI application is performing across all configured metrics.

<img
  title="AI Quality Evaluation dashboard showing evaluations summary, significant findings chart, and metric groups"
  alt="AI Quality Evaluation dashboard showing evaluations summary, significant findings chart, and metric groups"
  src="/images/ai-quality-evaluation_screenshot-full_quality-metrics-dashboard.webp"
/>

The dashboard shows:

- **Summary stats** at the top: Total evaluations run, number of quality findings, and an overall quality score percentage. Use this to gauge the health of your AI application at a glance—a high finding count or low quality score is your signal to investigate.
- **Significant quality findings chart**: Highlights which metrics are responsible for the most findings over your selected time range. Use this to prioritize—if Faithfulness or Jailbreak is spiking, those are the metrics worth drilling into first.
- **Metric groups** organized by risk type:
  - **Security & compliance**: Jailbreak, PII leakage, Prompt injection — flags inputs attempting to abuse or exploit the model.
  - **Content safety**: Bias, Toxicity — flags harmful or biased model outputs.
  - **Response accuracy**: Answer relevancy, Contextual relevancy, Faithfulness — flags responses that are off-topic, inconsistent, or factually wrong relative to the provided context.

Each metric card shows the percentage of evaluated messages flagged and the total finding count, with a trend indicator so you can see if things are improving or getting worse. You can filter the entire view by AI entity, model, and quality metric to isolate a specific service or time window.

### Investigate individual findings [#investigate]

Click <DoNotTranslate>**See details**</DoNotTranslate> on any metric to open its findings list. This is where you move from knowing *that* something is wrong to understanding *why*.

<img
  title="Metric findings detail view showing individual flagged messages with score, entity, model, user prompt, and LLM response"
  alt="Metric findings detail view showing individual flagged messages with score, entity, model, user prompt, and LLM response"
  src="/images/ai-quality-evaluation_screenshot-full_metric-findings-detail.webp"
/>

Each finding shows:

- **Date & time** of the interaction
- **Flagged for**: the metric name and the exact score (for example, `Jailbreak 1.0` or `Jailbreak 0.3`), so you can tell at a glance how severe the finding is
- **AI entity**, **Role**, and **Model**: which service, user role, and model was involved — useful for spotting if one entity or model is responsible for a disproportionate share of findings
- **User prompt** and **LLM response**: the actual content that triggered the flag

You can filter findings by AI entity, role, and model to narrow the list to the specific service or interaction type you want to investigate. Clicking on a finding opens the full AI trace in context so you can see the complete conversation and correlate the flagged message with other telemetry from that request.

## External evaluation (Bring your own evaluations) [#external-evaluation]

In addition to New Relic's built-in evaluation pipeline, you can send evaluation results computed by your own systems into New Relic for unified monitoring and alerting.

This is useful if you already have:

- An internal evaluation framework or custom judge models.
- Domain-specific quality metrics not covered by the built-in set.
- Offline or batch evaluation workflows.

### How external evaluation works [#how-external-works]

You use the New Relic External Evaluation API to push evaluation results for messages that were previously ingested via AI Monitoring.

The following Python example shows how to record a custom event for LLM analysis using the New Relic Python agent:

```python
newrelic.agent.record_custom_event(
        "LlmMessageCustomAnalysis",
        {
            "name": "Classification.Negative",
            "score": 0.6,
            "threshold": 0.5,
            "flagged": True,
            "trace.id": newrelic.agent.current_trace_id(),
            "span.id": newrelic.agent.current_span_id()
        }
    )
```
