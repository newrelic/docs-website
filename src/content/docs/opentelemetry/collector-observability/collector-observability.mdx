Monitor your [OpenTelemetry collector](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/collector/) health and performance with a dedicated APM UI experience. When your collector fails or misbehaves, it can cause a blackout of observability data, permanent data loss, or distorted insights. That's why we built Collector Observability—an APM experience tailored to the streaming work collectors perform. You can leverage the collector's [internal telemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/collector/internal-telemetry/) to see at a glance how each component of your collector is performing, so you can spot issues before they impact your observability pipeline.

This experience adapts to how your collector is deployed. A collector running on its own gets a streamlined, collector-focused view. A collector attached to an instrumented application—sharing the same <DNT>`service.name`</DNT> (and thus entity)—gets the full APM experience plus collector insight. See [Choose your collector's view mode](#view-modes) for details.

Go to [Get started with collector observability](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-setup) to make sure your collector is configured correctly for this new experience.

## View your collector in the UI [#view-ui]

### Explore the internal telemetry in the APM UI [#explore-ui]

To view your collector's internal telemetry, navigate to <DNT>**APM & Services > Services - OpenTelemetry > your_collector_service_name**</DNT> to explore the collector's entity.

Depending on the components your collector uses, some charts might not be populated. For example, if you don't process logs in your collector, the charts related to that signal will be empty.

### Choose your collector's view mode [#view-modes]

Collector Observability supports two view modes: <DNT>**Standalone Collector**</DNT> and <DNT>**Attached Collector**</DNT>.

* **Standalone Collector (recommended)**: a streamlined, collector-focused experience for a collector running on its own, represented by its own entity. It hides the standard <DNT>**APM Summary**</DNT> page and replaces it with a [view](#summary-page) focused on native collector telemetry.
* **Attached Collector**: the full APM experience plus collector capability. In this mode the APM experience continues to treat the application as the primary entity but adds a second tab on the <DNT>**APM Summary**</DNT> page for the <DNT>**Collector**</DNT>. This mode is intended for collectors that run together with the monitored application and share its <DNT>`service.name`</DNT>, regardless of how they're deployed (for example, as a sidecar or in the same VM). If your collector has a different <DNT>`service.name`</DNT>, which we recommend, we synthesize a dedicated entity for it and you can use <DNT>**Standalone Collector**</DNT> mode instead. See [the FAQ](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-faq#attached-limitations) for more details.

#### Set the mode [#set-mode]

Go to <DNT>**your collector's entity > Settings > Collector mode**</DNT> and choose one of:

* <DNT>**Automatic**</DNT> (default): If you set the <DNT>`otelCollector.viewModeIac`</DNT> tag described below, New Relic chooses the mode based on its value. If not, New Relic analyzes the entity's telemetry to determine the mode. To do this, it looks for attribute values that indicate the presence of a non-collector application under the same <DNT>`service.name`</DNT>.
* <DNT>**Standalone Collector**</DNT> or <DNT>**Attached Collector**</DNT>: pins the entity to that mode, overriding the tag and automatic classification.

If you manage a large fleet of collectors, leave the option configured as <DNT>**Automatic**</DNT> and set the entity tag <DNT>`otelCollector.viewModeIac`</DNT> to <DNT>`standalone`</DNT> or <DNT>`attached`</DNT> instead of visiting each entity's settings individually. See [the FAQ](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-faq#set-mode-at-scale) for ways to set tags at scale.

### Summary page [#summary-page]

The <DNT>**Collector**</DNT> view opens with six KPI (key performance indicator) tiles that give you an at-a-glance read on your collector's health: <DNT>**Items received**</DNT>, <DNT>**Items sent**</DNT>, <DNT>**Queue saturation**</DNT>, <DNT>**Receiver refused**</DNT>, <DNT>**Export failure rate**</DNT>, and <DNT>**Queue drops**</DNT>. Each tile shows the current value and its trend versus one hour ago.

<img
  title="Screenshot showing the KPI tiles"

/>

Click any KPI tile to open a detail view with its full chart, broken down by telemetry signal (metrics, logs, spans), plus related telemetry charts for additional context. See the [KPI reference](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-kpis) for details on each KPI and related charts.

If you wonder what happened to the standard APM Summary page, see the [FAQ](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-faq#summary-page-transactions).

Below the KPI tiles, the rest of the collector's telemetry is organized into collapsible sections you can expand as needed:

* **Collector and Errors**: error logs and running instances.
* **Receivers** and **Exporters**: per-component charts for each pipeline stage.
* **Processors**: per-processor charts, including a dedicated chart for [memorylimiter](https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry-collector/tree/v0.161.0/processor/memorylimiterprocessor) due to its unique failure mode.
* **Batch**: batching behavior, requires [batchprocessor](https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry-collector/tree/v0.161.0/processor/batchprocessor).
* **Process**: the collector process's own resource usage—CPU, RSS memory, and heap.
* **Infrastructure**: infrastructure relationships and metrics, if configured (see [configuration examples](/docs/opentelemetry/collector-observability/collector-observability-setup#examples)).
